Chapter 1Rain: three steps, a circle on the map, and proof

In one line

Rain is now three clicks: a past heavy rain → the areas it hit → one area, where the map zooms into a circle and lists its flooded streets one by one, each with Take action and its follow-up, and every figure shows where it came from.

1.1 The three steps (the same on all four tabs)

  1. Past heavy rains, newest first. Each row: the dates, the district average rainfall (for example "27 mm average"), how many streets (or roads, wards, lines) it hit, and the area hit most.
  2. One rain: a "Rain measured" box, then the areas it hit, most first. Each area is a zone (Metro: a line).
  3. One area: the map flies to a dashed circle with the area's name; the panel lists the places one by one. Open one to see why it is listed (the complaints or reports behind it) and its action tracker: assign to a department with a due date and a note, follow what the department reports with photos, verify, and see whether the next comparable rain proved the fix.

Tabs: Flooding, Traffic, Power, Metro. Satellite water seen in the chosen rain is drawn on every tab and named in the map key. Everything that crowded the old panel (three KPIs, insight sentence, "also in this rain" chips, bulk send, repeated "review and send") was removed.

1.2 Proof for every figure

Example (24 Nov rain, read on 29 Nov)

"District average 27 mm from 26 automatic rain gauges (20 inside Chennai); the wettest, Pozhichalur, recorded 68 mm. IMD station: 35 mm." Sources: Chennai Flood Monitor (TN WRD CFM-DSS) and India Meteorological Department, each a link to the official site. An area shows its own rain, for example "29 mm (CFM-DSS gauges, 3 wards)". A street lists its complaints, each with an Open button.

1.3 Only real heavy rains

The pipeline's list of heavy-rain days included days when the gauges recorded almost nothing. 16 November showed "0 mm" with more than 100 flooding reports: the IMD station recorded 0 mm and 25 gauges averaged 0.1 mm (wettest 2 mm). Those were ordinary waterlogging complaints, not a rain.

Why this, not that

A rain is listed only if the gauges did not find it dry: a district average of at least 15 mm, or one gauge at IMD's 64.5 mm heavy-rain mark. Four days were dropped (18 Jul, 28 Aug, 16 Nov, 27 Nov). Showing them would have made the "proof" contradict itself.

1.4 How the circle is drawn

The circle's centre is the average position of the area's places; its radius is the farthest place plus 300 m, between 550 m and 6 km. The camera frames the circle; a place opened from the list or the map gets the camera at street level.

Be honest about

Power-cut hours and train delays in heavy rain are generated demonstration logs (marked as such in the sources). Flooding, satellite water, gauges and traffic records are real. Two September rains had only 6 gauges reporting, so they show "Only 6 gauges reported" instead of an average.

Say it in the review

"Every number on the Rain screen opens to its source: the gauges that measured it, and the complaint that put the street on the list. And we removed days the gauges found dry, so a rain on our screen is a rain."
